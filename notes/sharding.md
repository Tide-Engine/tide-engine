# Shards: one world across servers

## Border and recovery audit (2026-10-24)

The owner reported further disconnects, a zero online count and a car being
pushed back at an apparent border. The production checkpoint contained a live
car with `Roster.online == 0`; replaying its retained records also reproduced
a negative count after the final departure. Raw captures stay in ignored build
storage. The invisible town edge was a separate Earth projection bug
(`notes/docs-site.md`, Town-edge collision correction).

The audit reproduced and fixed these engine failures:

- A needed neighbour that timed out stayed in `clients` forever, so `follow`
  never reached it again. Lost neighbours now release their failed endpoint
  for one update, then go through admission again with the original cookie.
  That absent update lets `update_admissions` discard the detached endpoint.
  Refusals and kicks are terminal. `outlive_home` no longer hides a home kick
  by selecting another connected shard; its reason and message reach the caller.
- A world's journal could contain a departure newer than the coordinator's
  checkpoint. Loading replayed the departure, then the coordinator sent it
  again after its absence grace. Joins newer than the checkpoint also lacked
  their saved cookie. With checkpoints enabled, connection changes are now
  batched and checkpointed before the root gets their PlayerJoined/PlayerLeft
  notifications. Membership/cookie tracking waits while that checkpoint is
  gathered or kept, so its header and captured worlds agree.
- Checkpoint envelope 5 stores the pending notifications with that roster.
  Loading reissues them. Generated `tide_shard_state.members` is a bounded
  bitset in match state, updated before dispatching those notifications, so
  replay or reissue cannot deliver the same logical arrival/departure twice.
  It uses existing checked list/COW operations and adds no runtime function
  called by generated code. The wire protocol remains 18; the internal world
  layout and simulation identity change. Older releases retain their own code
  and checkpoint reader; their raw saves are not this new layout.
- A restarted root reconciles committed membership, leaving uncommitted
  notifications behind their checkpoint barrier. A shard restarted while a
  checkpoint is gathering is asked for its replacement snapshot.
- Anonymous joins could take slots reserved for recovery when the game was
  full. The server's reuse search now excludes admitted slots. Global
  departures revoke admissions on every shard (a zero-cookie admission
  instruction); `tide_server_unadmit` frees an unused slot's old identity so
  the next player's cookie can be admitted there, while live connections and
  migration reservations remain intact.

`shards_players_test.c` now covers repeated crossings, reversals and a
four-region corner at 60/144/240 updates per second, with up to 100 ms one-way
latency, 50 ms jitter and 15% loss: one visible avatar, never two authoritative
owners, stable identity and continued verification. A seven-second outage of
an already-followed neighbour must recover before crossing into it. Further
regressions cover a kick with a healthy neighbour, reserved-slot rejection and
reuse, and invalid lifecycle player IDs without changing the world.

Earth's tests cover a departure replayed just before a crash, joins cut both
before and after their first game tick, a root-only restart, original cookies
and avatars, repeated lifecycle notifications, and ordinary leave/rejoin churn.
The pre-fix departure, neighbour, kick and reservation regressions all failed.
Checkpoint-enabled worlds now pay for one checkpoint per batch of membership
changes, in addition to periodic/shape checkpoints. This is durability work
on joins/leaves, not an optimization of steady tick traffic.

Validation: warning-free native/web debug builds; all 2,621 enabled native
and 2,479 enabled web CTests pass, including the 12 shard-player and 15 Earth
checks, native Vulkan/OpenGL and web WebGL/D3D11/WebGPU. Both retain the
existing disabled MicroDuck right-turn limitation. Optimized native and web
Earth builds, native town/car captures, muted WebGL/WebGPU landing/walking
checks and the 64-bot dynamic-merge run pass (`notes/benchmarks.md`). Release
ordinary/split pins agree with debug/WASI. The companion hosting service's
Svelte check/build and all 58 tests pass against the staged compiler/runtime,
including real room border crossing, host loss and checkpoint/journal recovery.
Node is 24.22.1. Android, other operating systems, MinGW and sanitizers remain
CI coverage; no workflow was manually dispatched. Deployment evidence follows
once the merged build is published.

## The language side

The owner's rule (2026-10-23): shards aren't a game's concern, and what a game declares is a fact about the game, not an instruction about shards. A game writes `shards` and `reach`; a C library's package writes `[Native]` and `[Together]`. They replaced, the same day, `shardRange`, `[Partition]`, `[PerShard]` (singleton and field), `[Shared]`, `[Ghosts]`, `[GoesWith]` and `Shards.moves`.

- `settings { shards = true; reach = 64; }` (`check_settings` in `compiler/src/check.c`; internally still `prog->shard_range`): `reach` is required with `shards` and means nothing without it; both errors say why.
- **Where an entity is**: `[Position]` vectors of two or three axes on match components (`note_position` from the `[Position]` check, into `prog->positions`). `tide_shard_where` tries the game's in declaration order, then the packages' (`shard_position`), then `Transform`'s `localPosition` (a root's is its world position). `check_shard_fields` makes every one in a sharded game have as many axes (`position_axes`). A spawn of an archetype with any of them, or `Transform`, is placed and gets no `tide_follows`.
- `Shards.Owns(entity)` and `Shards.delay` (`compiler/src/builtins.c`): `tide_shard_owns_now` (the world the thread's tick or frame registered with `tide_shard_use`) and the thread-local `tide_shard_delay_now`. In a game that isn't sharded `Shards.Owns` compiles to `true` (`gen_builtin_call`), so a library's loop over other shards' entities compiles away.
- `ensure_shards` adds two hidden declarations: the sparse component `tide_ghost { int source; }`, which every match archetype can have (`derive_archetypes`), and the singleton `tide_shard_state`: `shard` (the id + 1, 0 for a world that's no shard), `role`, `sent` (the last number its messages had), `acked`, `next`/`end` (the entity index range it gives from), `singletons` (a bit for each of the game's singletons it owns), `moves` (arrivals and departures, which C libraries read with `tide_shard_moves_now`), and lists of plain numbers and bytes: `regions` (7 doubles each: shard, box lo, box hi), `ranges`, `heard` (per sender, the last number applied), `pins` (borrowed entities kept until a tick), `asked`, `gone`, `held`, `outbox`, `waits` and `shared`. They're match state like any, so snapshots, hashes, packing and the clients' worlds keep them.
- Every per-entity match system and handler is filtered `without tide_ghost` before archetypes are derived: a shard runs code for what it owns, and reads ghosts through queries and `World`.
- A per-entity match system taking a game singleton `mut` warns in a sharded game (`warn_shard_singletons`): each region's shard would borrow it from the root in turn, every tick. Code for no entity runs on the root, which has the singletons, and doesn't warn. Fixture: `compiler/tests/warnings/shard_singleton_rows.tide`.
- **`[Native]`** (`field.native`, the last member of `field`, so positional initializers end in a `false`): on a match component, a number or a bool with no default, 0 on a ghost and on a record taken in; on a match singleton, anything, and the singleton is `decl.native_state`. Such a singleton isn't shared (`shard_shares`): each shard keeps its `[Native]` fields, and its other fields are the root's, which `singletons_of` appends to the root's singleton ghosts (`tide_shard_put_shared`), a region's `GHOSTS` receive keeps (`tide_shard_state.shared`) and every tick starts by applying (`tide_shard_apply_shared`), in the world's own heap (`tide_world_tick_on` sets it first: until 2026-10-23 it read the list in whatever heap the thread had last, which a session, running servers and clients on one thread, made a client's every tick diverge as soon as a game had such a singleton; the box tests, servers alone, were lucky). A region's code that wrote those fields (code for an entity) sends them to the root at the tick's end when they differ from the root's it holds (`tide_shard_send_shared`, `TIDE_SHARD_SHARED`, numbered), and keeps them as the root's meanwhile; the root takes them as its own (`tide_shard_take_shared`), and they reach every shard as the root's do. Two regions writing at once: the one the root applies last. `check_shard_fields` makes those other fields plain values. Code for no entity taking one runs on every shard when it's declared in the same package (`shard_everywhere`, `same_package`): the library's own; a game's runs once, on the root, so its writes to the settings are the root's and reach every shard.
- **`[Together]`** on a match component's `Entity` field (`prog->together`, `struct field_mark`): handoff keeps what it names with the entity that has it (below).
- The visibility directory (`tide_visibility_state`) is shared like a game singleton (`shard_shares`), and `tide_visibility_create_group`/`_set_group`/`_destroy_group` call `tide_shard_singleton_may` in a sharded game, so a region's change waits and borrows it. Its ghost and take code releases its maps through `put_singleton_release`, as builtin singletons have no `tide_release_` of their own.
- A machine's home can end under it (`outlive_home`, `engine/src/shard_client.c`): a region merged into another stops once what it owned has gone there, but the machine moves its home only once its verified world shows that, which corrections can put a second behind. Before 2026-10-24 it went offline (`TIDE_DISCONNECT_SERVER_LEFT`), found by `bench_earth --split 4 --busiest 25`, with the regions merging while few were in. Now it plays on in the shard its route last named as its home (`wanted`), or another it follows, or else comes back in at the root with its cookie, which every route reaches. Making the retiring shard wait for its players instead kept a drained shard running that the rest of the protocol takes as gone (`shards_merge_quiet_regions`' index check failed).
- An entity's record (`tide_shard_put_one`, `tide_shard_take_one`) writes its components with the recipient-state codec, which leaves out `Visibility`'s hidden `overrides` (who may have each restricted component: a recipient isn't told). `shard_hidden_fields` writes and reads them too, after the rest of the `Visibility`. Until 2026-10-24 they were left out, so a component restricted by `visibility.Set<T>(audience)` went to everyone once its entity had changed shards, until the game set it again (found by Earth's players' walkers).

## Generated code (`compiler/src/shard_codegen.inc`, `shard_messages_codegen.inc`, `shard_world_codegen.inc`)

- **Ownership.** `tide_shard_owned(w, e)`: alive here and no ghost, spawned this tick, destroyed here (`tide_entity_died_here`), or confirmed gone by its owner (`gone`). `tide_shard_may` is what `World.Set`, `Add`, `Remove`, `Destroy`, `Unload`, `Snap` and parenting call first: false, noting the running code's identity and what it touched (`tide_shard_note`), for anything else. A `mut` shared singleton is checked as a system or handler starts (`tide_shard_singleton_may`; a singleton is noted as the entity `{number, TIDE_ENTITY_TEMPORARY}`).
- **Identity.** A system sets `tide_shard_code` (1 + its index) and `tide_shard_this` as its body starts, and returns at once when the tick's skip set has it. Event deliveries do the same in the dispatcher (1 + systems + the event's number, and each code's occurrence count this run); a skipped delivery is parked (`tide_shard_park_<Event>`: its code, target, the tick it first tried, a command's sender, what it touched and the event's value) instead of run. `Destroyed`'s handlers are noted by its code and the entity the destroy is of (`tide_shard_dying`, set by the shard's `destroy_now`, occurrence 0), and what waits is the destroy (below). A task's resume is noted by its kind's code (after the events'), its owner and its start order as the occurrence; one the skip set has is left as it was, due again next tick, as a system's row is. Code with no identity, code 0, can't wait: its changes across a border are dropped and counted (every system, delivery, destroy and task resume has one).
- **The tick** (`gen_shard_tick`): `tide_world_tick_on` copies the world, runs the tick (`tide_world_tick_once`), and when anything was noted, takes the noted runs into the skip set, restores the copy and runs again, up to `TIDE_SHARD_RUNS` (8) runs; code 0 noting anything ends it at once. Both end with what was noted dropped and counted (`tide_shard_dropped`). Each run is deterministic, so a shard's clients do the same with nothing sent. The local event log is rewound with the world. After the last run, `tide_shard_after`: what waiting code needs is `held`, what it touched and doesn't own is asked for (`BORROW`, or `BORROW_SINGLETON` to the root), requests waiting to be lent are lent when they can be, borrowed singletons whose pins ran out go back to the root, and entities that left the region move (`tide_shard_handoff`).
- **Lending and priority.** A shard lends what it owns when asked, unless it's pinned (borrowed less than `TIDE_SHARD_PIN_TICKS`, 30 ticks, ago) or `held`, and the asker's number is higher than its own: then the request waits (`TIDE_SHARD_LEND` in `waits`). The lower shard always gets what it asks for, so code waiting on several shards' entities can't wait on each other forever; the first end-to-end run, before this rule, had two banks swapping places every 30 ticks for good.
- **Waiting deliveries** run first in `tide_apply_commands` (`tide_shard_resume`), each in full through its event's dispatcher, once this shard owns all it touched, with `Shards.delay` the ticks since it first tried.
- **Destroys that wait.** A shard's `destroy_now` asks `tide_shard_destroy_waits` first: when the skip set has `Destroyed`'s code for the entity, the destroy doesn't happen. It's parked like a delivery (with the entity among what it needs, as it can be lent meanwhile) and the entity's tree is marked with the hidden sparse `tide_dying` (`since`: the tick it first waited), which every match archetype can have and every per-entity match system and handler is filtered without, as for ghosts. `tide_shard_resume` unmarks the tree and destroys it again, which runs its handlers or waits again. The tag travels with the entity's records. Handoff leaves `held` entities where they are, so a dying entity stays until it's destroyed; one lent away comes back by position once its pin runs out.
- **Moving.** `tide_shard_handoff` sends each owned root whose position is in another region, a quarter of the range past its own, unless pinned: an `ARRIVE` with the tree's records, and the tree stays as ghosts of the shard it went to. The root removes it unless it has a region of its own (`tide_shard_regional`: a world of one region, or one merged back into the root), when it holds ghosts near its region like any shard.
- **Groups.** Handoff first ties entities into groups (`tide_shard_ties_of`, a union-find over a hash of entity keys, `tide_shard_tie`): every live entity, owned or a ghost, joins what each of its `[Together]` fields names, and an entity of an archetype with no position (`shard_placed`) and no parent joins the first entity its fields name that's alive here (`shard_named`, `tide_shard_first_named_<archetype>`: the archetype's components' `Entity` fields in order, the engine's aside). A group's lead is its member with a position and the lowest key, so every shard that sees the group picks the same one, and a closed ring of joints has one lead rather than each member waiting on the next. A member whose lead is a ghost here goes to that ghost's shard; one whose lead is this shard's stays (`stays`/`led_by`) and goes with it in the same tick if it goes. The lead goes by its position. A shard whose lead stays asks for every member it only has a ghost of (`BORROW`, `asked`), so a joint's far body comes to its joint's shard. An entity with no position whose first named entity isn't here and isn't known gone goes `TO_OWNER` of it; with none, by its spawner (`tide_follows`).
- **Followers.** A command records the entity whose code recorded it (`tide_command.spawner`: `tide_shard_this` in a shard's code, null outside its ticks, so nothing from another world or test leaks in), and a spawn of an archetype with no position (no `[Position]` vector and no `Transform`) and no scene of its own gets the hidden sparse `tide_follows { of }` naming it. Handoff sends an unpositioned root to where `of` is: to the shard a ghost of it came from, or else `TO_OWNER` of `of` (`tide_shard_send_tree_with`, which keeps no ghost); the orchestrator notes the tree where it routed it, and sends one whose `of` is no shard's back. One whose `of` this shard owns, or that died here, stays. A follower that later gets a position goes by it.
- **Tasks** go with the entity they belong to (`tide_shard_send_tasks` after the tree in an `ARRIVE`, `gen_shard_tasks`): the tree's waiting tasks, in the order they started, each its kind, wake, owner, scene and frame, taken out of the sender's tables and started on the receiver in that order (`tide_task_order`). Frames go through value codecs appended to the others after `gen_tasks`, as a frame's fields are only known once its code is generated. Ghost records carry none.
- **Records.** An entity is its size, handle, archetype (the same build on every shard numbers them alike), snaps, scene, each component (sparse ones after whether they're there) through the value codecs recipient projections use, and its hierarchy links. A `[Native]` field is set to 0 as each value is decoded, and `tide_shard_mark_one` zeroes them on an entity becoming a ghost in place. `tide_shard_take_one` reads every value before changing anything, installs the handle (`tide_entity_install`), puts the row in its archetype or updates it in place, and sets the ghost tag; a ghost of something this shard owns is skipped.
- **Events** to an entity this shard doesn't own go to the shard that does (`tide_shard_forward_<Event>`, an `EVENT` routed by entity) and run there. Batched and split event dispatch is off in sharded games (`generating_shards`) so every delivery passes the check.
- **Indices.** Spawns take handles from the shard's ranges (`tide_shard_create`, through `tide_new_entity_by` and `tide_tick_systems.create` for systems that settle spawns on threads). An index is listed free only when the shard gave it (`tide_shard_free_slot`); an entity that left, or another shard's, is forgotten (`tide_entity_forget`, `TIDE_ARCHETYPE_AWAY` when it lives on), so no index is ever two entities. An entity destroyed away from home gives its index back: `tide_shard_free_slot` posts `FREED` (`TIDE_SHARD_TO_RANGE`), which the coordinator carries to the shard whose range has the index (`range_owner`) and takes out of the directory; there `tide_entity_release` lists the index free (its generation moved on), unless a later handle of it is already alive there. The directory now holds only entities alive away from their range's shard: one back home is taken out (`note_tree`). `check_away` in `shards_test.c` checks every shard's away indices are its entities alive elsewhere; without the release a busy run had 33 away against 27.
- **Views** (`gen_shard_views`, the frame in `gen_game_api`): a sharded game's header has `tide_frame_shards(more, count)`, which the host (`tide_host_game.frame_shards`, from `tide_session_views`) calls before each `tide_frame` with the other shards' worlds this machine follows, for that frame alone (`tide_view_more`, cleared as the frame ends; `tide_view_home` is the frame's own). A view for each entity runs again for each of them (`tide_view_switch`: `tide_view_drawing`, `Shards.Owns` through `tide_shard_use`, and the match heap, and the confirmed world's where a view takes `verified`), and its row loop leaves out what `tide_view_hides` says another world draws (`g->view_rows`, in `gen_chunk`): a ghost of what a drawn world owns, or of what none owns that an earlier drawn world (the home's first) has a copy of. The source shard isn't asked: what's on its way between two is a ghost in both for a moment, and the first rule, by the source, left it undrawn then. A view's query (`gen_query_fill`) has every drawn world's rows: `_most` and `_in` per world, the latter after `tide_view_switch`, copying what a row of a world other than the one the view runs on holds of its heap into the scratch area (`gen_scratch_value`: text, lists, maps and sets, through their elements; a grid can't hold cells in the scratch area, so it's copied into the local state's heap, `tide_grid_copy_from` (engine/src/grid.c) with every chunk, kept in `tide_view_grids` and released as the frame ends, `tide_view_drop_grids`). Local fields are kept while any drawn world has their entity (`gen_forget`). `World` reads (`Get`, `Has`, `TryGet`, `Parent`, `LocalToWorld`, `ParentToWorld`; `view_reads` in codegen.c), a view's and any function's or handler's in a sharded game, go through `tide_view_get_<Component>` and the like: the world drawn that owns the entity, else the one the view runs on when it has a copy, else the first with one (`tide_view_world_of`); a value from another world has its heap's text, lists, maps and sets copied as a query's are. In a tick no other world is drawn, so they're the world's own reads, a branch later. With no other world given, the frame is what it was.
- **What reaches far** (`Shards.Spans(entity, center, size)`, `Shards.on`): a run notes spans on its tick (`tide_shard_span_note`, under the lock), and after the last run `tide_shard_spanning` keeps, sorted by key and merged, those reaching past the entity's position by more than `reach` (`tide_shard_state.spanned`, keys, and `spans`, 6 doubles each), every tick anew. The border (`tide_shard_border`, the agent's) shares an owned tree whose position is near the target or whose span reaches it (`tide_shard_near`, `tide_shard_spans_into`). `Shards.on` is `TIDE_SHARDS_ON`, a constant. Box3D and Box2D note each owned static body's box (`b3t_span`/`b2t_span`, the body's AABB) in `CopyBodies`; Newton notes a plane's body as reaching everywhere (1e30 across). Test: `tide_box3d_shards`' floor 70 m long placed at x = -25, under a crate falling onto it at x = 6 on the right: it lands (fails without the span).
- **Entities with no position** were never shared by a region (the border took only what has a position, and the root's unpositioned ones): a part on a body by the border had no copy on the other side. The border now shares one where the first entity it names is near (`tide_shard_first_named_<a>`). Test: the post by the border in `tide_box3d_shards` and its part, which the right holds a copy of (fails without).
- **`Shards.Of(entity)`** (2026-10-24, Provisional, for the Earth demo on the docs' home page): `tide_shard_of`, generated beside `tide_view_world_of`: of the world `tide_shard_use` registered (the drawn world in a view, through `tide_shard_world_now`, `engine/src/shard.c`), the world drawn that owns the entity (`tide_view_world_of`), then its own shard (`tide_shard_self`) or, for a copy, the ghost's `source`; -1 when it isn't alive there. `check.c` refuses it in a match system or handler (`shards_of_in_match`); a game that isn't sharded compiles it to 0 (`gen_builtin_call`). Test: `shards_a_machine_watches_an_area_far_from_its_player` adds up `Shards.Of` of each marker a view for each and a query drew: 2 (the right's) for the left's copy of the near one, 8 for four from the right while watching, and the avatar 1 then 2 as it crosses; with the copy's `source` left out (each world's own shard), it fails.
- **Embedded** (`tide/embed.h`, `tidec --embed`): the view packet is every drawn world's as the frame's views are (`gen_embed_worlds_begin`/`_end` in embed_codegen.inc around its entity and component rows, each row under `tide_view_hides`, parents by `tide_view_parent_of`, places sized by the largest world); `platform/src/embed.c`'s `write_view` hands it the other worlds again (`frame_shards`), and the packet clears them, as a frame does. The embed host takes `--shards` and `--shard-width` as `tide run` does (`embed_options`, a bad one left out). Test: `tide_platform_embed_shards` (six rocks over two regions, no player, a view watching all: each in the packet once; it fails without `write_view`'s handing over).
- **Local handlers and availability.** Every followed shard's client records what its ticks send, and the shard client hands each log to the host, the home's first, but for their Shown and Hidden (`TIDE_HEARD_AVAILABILITY`): an event is recorded where it's delivered, so each comes once. Until this, the shard client's logs were never marked `taken`, so no client recorded anything and a sharded game's local handlers heard nothing at all; they now follow the host's log. Shown and Hidden come from the frame (`gen_availability_shards`): each drawn world is compared with its copy from the last frame, kept by shard (`tide_kept_worlds`, predicted and verified), a shard no longer drawn with nothing, and a Shown passes only for an entity no other drawn world had, a Hidden only for one no other has now, each once (by entity, event and handlers). Genuine destructions are of entities no drawn world has. Which listeners an entity satisfies is each world's to say: one another world has counts as available, whatever its components there.
- **Corrections** (`[Position]` smoothing): every followed shard's client reports its own (`tide_client_set_beside` marks all but the home's, `tide_view_correction.beside`); a beside one corrects only entities its world owns and never starts the rest over. In a sharded game no ghost is corrected, and a correction stays while any drawn world has its entity (`tide_corrections_prune`).
- **Starting.** A region's world starts empty (`tide_shard_starting_empty` skips the start scene and Main's return), at the root's tick. `PlayerJoined`/`PlayerLeft` aren't sent by a shard's session: the root sends them when the orchestrator says (`PLAYER`). A region never ends the match.

## The session (`engine/src/session.c`, `PROTOCOL` 18)

- `tide_server_inject` queues a shard message for the server's coming ticks; `dispatch_injected` applies them before the tick (`tide_shard_game.receive`), after the tick's commands, and writes them in the frame (`FRAME_SHARD`): inline up to `TIDE_COMMAND_BYTES`, else published as a big command is and named by its download, the tick waiting for every player to have it. At most `TIDE_SHARD_MESSAGES_PER_TICK` (64) a tick, up to `TIDE_SHARD_MESSAGE_LIMIT` (64 MiB) each.
- Clients apply them from the frame at the same point. A frame with shard messages is never "as predicted" (`as_predicted`): before that, a region shard's clients asked for the world again every tick.
- A shard message over `TIDE_COMMAND_BYTES` is published and its players download it; a sharded game's clients report download progress (the `MSG_CLIENT` and `on_client` fields) whether or not the game has commands. Before, a game without commands never acknowledged one, and the server held it and everything after it for good: a merge's trees froze `shards_split_a_busy_region_and_merge_quiet_ones` until this.
- `tide_server_injected_message` and `tide_server_drop_injected` let the orchestrator read and drop messages still waiting (a reload's quiet check and dropped ghosts).
- Admission: a region's server is made closed, and takes only players the orchestrator admitted (`tide_server_admit`: a player and the cookie the root gave them), as that player, so a player is one PlayerID in every shard. `tide_server_connected` and `tide_server_cookie` are what the orchestrator reads to see who's in the world.
- `tide_session_start` of a sharded game (`start_shards`) runs a `tide_shards` in the session over loopback, its servers at endpoints 1 to 512 and this machine's clients from 513 (`SHARDS_HERE`), with `shard_world`/`shard_split`/`shard_most` from the session's desc, and this machine's player a `tide_shard_client`. The session's world, events and status are its home's; `status.server` is true. `tide_session_migrate` is false for a sharded session: a sharded match starts over.
- `tide_session_join` of a sharded game makes a `tide_shard_client` of what it joined, taken as the root. When what it joined is a hosting admission's transport (`tide_hosting_client_of`: the host's, which outlives the match), each other shard its route names is reached as an admission beside it (`shard_join_reach`, `tide_hosting_client_beside`: over the same connection, asking for that shard's match with a fresh nonce), its client made once admitted (`shard_handed`: the admission keeps its endpoint until then and isn't made again). Joined any other way, only the first shard is ever played in.

## The journal and recovering from a crash

Visibility journals (2026-10-24): the server now records private shard
messages and commands in a separate full replay frame, even though recipient
frames omit them. Earth's hosted checkpoint exposed the missing messages;
its four-region test now replays every recorded tick through player joins,
town travel and leaves, then checks each shard's complete hash. Existing
records that omitted effects remain unrecoverable. Details and full
native/web validation: notes/netcode.md, A server's journal.

- **A server's journal** (`tide_server_desc.journal`, `journal_tick` in `engine/src/session.c`): after each tick, a record of the tick's frame as players get it (joins and leaves, changed inputs, commands, shard messages) and the bytes of each big command or message the frame names by download (`frame_names_each` finds them; a named download the server no longer has aborts, as it's kept until its frame goes). `tide_replay` runs the tick again on the world from before it, as a client applies a frame, and checks the world's hash against the frame's. Inputs come as deltas from what the world keeps, which a checkpointed world has. A server's tick counter starts at 0 whatever world it starts from; `tide_replay_tick` reads a record's.
- **The orchestrator's journal** (`tide_shards_desc.journal`, `flush`, `checkpoint`, `checkpoint_seconds`): every shard's records, numbered on across restarts (`ticks_before`, patched into a record's first word); `flush` once an update after the servers ticked and before anything is carried, so no message leaves a shard before the ticks that made it are kept.
- **Acknowledging** (`acknowledge`): a sender lets go of a message only once every shard has applied everything it was handed (`drained`: no server has injected messages waiting) and those ticks were flushed. Until this change, carrying a message acknowledged it at once, so a crash between a receiver taking a message and applying it lost it.
- **Reshaping** (`reshape`): more entity ranges, splits, merges and retired shards ending happen only when drained, and any of them (or `checkpoint_seconds` passing) is followed at once by a checkpoint. So no record kept after a checkpoint depends on a shape it doesn't have, and an orchestrator message on its way at a checkpoint is one loading sends again (map, ranges, retirement) or rebuilds (ghosts, routes).
- **Recovering** (`tide_shards_recover`): the checkpoint, then each shard's records with ticks from the one it saved, through `tide_replay`; a gap, bytes that aren't a record or a hash that differs fail. What was carried and not applied is in its sender's outbox, sent again; what was applied, receivers drop by number. Players of the world have 20 seconds to come back as themselves (`PLAYER_AWAY_SECONDS`, also for a player who drops out briefly: `PlayerLeft` only after it), admitted with their cookies.
- **A dedicated server's journal** (`--shard-journal <folder>`, `platform/src/serve.c`): `checkpoint` (written beside as `checkpoint.next`, synced, then moved over it) and `shard-<id>.log` (a u32 size and a record, over and over), synced by `flush`; a checkpoint removes every log. Started with a folder holding a checkpoint, the server recovers from it (a record a crash cut short is passed over) and checkpoints at once, so nothing is written after a cut-short record. A journal that can't be written stops the server.

## Players (`tide/shard_client.h`, `engine/src/shard_client.c`; the orchestrator's `track_players`, `route_players`)

- A player joins the world at the root. Once any shard has them connected, the orchestrator admits them everywhere with the root's cookie and has the root send `PlayerJoined` (`PLAYER`); `PlayerLeft` once no shard has them.
- Each shard reports, for every player, how many entities they own there and the box around them (`tide_shard_game.presence`). A player's home is the shard owning the most; they follow every shard owning any, and every region within the range of their entities' box. Only what each shard has is gathered (`sighting`s, sorted by player), and the regions near a player's entities are looked for among the neighbours of the regions owning them (`find_neighbours`: regions within 1.25 ranges of each other, found again when `tell_map` changes the map), so no table of players by shards is made. A `ROUTE` (the home and, for each followed shard, its id and its host's match) goes to every shard when that changes, so every client of every shard has every player's route as match state (`tide_shard_game.route`).
- `tide_shard_client` keeps a `tide_client` per followed shard, all with the same player and cookie and the same `sample`, so input reaches every shard owning any of the player's entities. It moves its home to the route's once that shard's client is connected: local code sees that world (views draw every followed one, through `tide_shard_client_views`), and only the home's local events go on to the host (each client records into its own log). A client the route no longer names is closed, never the home.
- A player's avatar spawned by `PlayerJoined` on the root moves to its region by position like anything else, and the player's home follows it.
- **While an entity moves**, no shard owns it: the sender's presence lost it in the report its ARRIVE is in, and the receiver's has it only once a tick applied it. A route then lost the receiver for an update or two, and the machine closed its client of it and joined it again a moment later, a whole world's download. Now `note_tree` marks the receiver's next batch (`cshard.arrival`), and while any ARRIVE is carried and not applied (`moving`), `route_players` adds the shards a player's route had (`roster.follows`) to the new one: a route only lets a shard go once nothing is on its way. A busy world follows a shard a little longer.
- **Watching an area** (`Session.Watch(center, size)`, `Session.Unwatch()`, local code): `tide_request_watch` turns it into a box and asks the host (`TIDE_REQUEST_WATCH`, `tide_session_request.area`) only when it changed (`tide_watch_told`); the host gives it to `tide_session_watch`, which holds it across matches and hands it to the shard client every update (`tide_shard_client_watch`). The client finds the shards whose regions are within `reach` of the box in the map every world has (`tide_shard_game.regions`: `tide_shard_state.regions` and, beside it, `matches`, the match each region's host knows it by) and follows them beside its route's (`add_watched`, up to `FOLLOWS_MOST` in all), closing those neither names. Nothing goes to the server: every shard admitted the player when it joined. For the match to be there, MAP and CONFIG carry each region's match (`write_regions`), and a match a host sets after the map was told (`tide_shard_coordinator_set_match`, which comes after its START) sends the map again at the next step (`map_due`), unless a new shape waits for its checkpoint, which tells it.

## Dedicated servers (`platform/src/serve.c`, `engine/src/hosting.c`)

- `tide_serve` of a sharded game runs a `tide_shards` over its `tide_hosting`, each shard a hosted match (`tide_shards_hosting_start`/`_stop`): kept running with no players (`tide_hosting_keep`), the root the host's front (`tide_hosting_front`), so a request for match 0 joins the world. `--shards N` (regions along x), `--shard-width W` (each region's width, 16 times `reach` without it) and `--shard-most N` (`most_entities`); they're errors for a game that isn't sharded. `--max-matches` must take every shard, splits included.
- One connection carries every shard a player follows, UDP or a room alike (so web players follow shards too): the hosting envelope routes each datagram by its admission's route id, and admissions beside one another share their transport (`host_link`: whichever is updated reads it, each datagram goes to the admission it's for, the last to go closes it). A region's match is open (`tide_hosting_open`, set by `tide_shards_hosting_start`): a join request for it needs no credential or `authorize`, as its closed server takes only players the root admitted, by cookie.

## The orchestrator: agents and a coordinator (`tide/shard_agent.h`, `tide/shard_coordinator.h`, `tide/shards.h`)

The orchestrator is two halves that talk in bytes, so the same code runs in one process and across machines (the hosting service's).

- **A shard's agent** (`engine/src/shard_agent.c`) sits beside its server. After each update it reports (`TIDE_SHARD_REPORT`): the last instruction batch applied, the shard's journal tick, messages handed and not yet applied, its status, the players connected (PlayerID and cookie), each player's presence, its world's outbox messages since the last report (`reported`), and its ghost messages for each target: what changed of what it owns near the target's region since it last sent it, by each tree's bytes' hash, and drops (the root also sends its unpositioned entities and its singletons, to every region). Ghost diffing lives here, as only this shard has its world; the maps of what it sent each target are the agent's (`sent`), with the bytes it last sent of each tree (`bases`). A tree it sent before, whose bytes it knows (not after a restart, a rebuild or an arrival, which mark them unknown), goes as what changed of those bytes (GHOSTS' form 1: runs of bytes the same and bytes changed, XOR the base, `write_changes`), and the receiver, server and clients alike, packs its ghost to get the base (`tide_shard_take_changes`): `[Native]` values are packed as 0, as every receiver holds them, so a ghost packs to what it was sent. The base's hash is checked; one that differs changes nothing and asks the sender for the tree whole (`TIDE_SHARD_STALE`, which the sender's agent reads as it's handed, `note_stale`). Before this every changed tree went whole, every tick, in every frame of the receiver's players: in `tide_box3d_shards` that was 419,326 bytes, now 97,371 (`tide_shards_ghost_bytes`, checked under 150,000). `shards_send_what_changed_of_a_ghost_against_what_it_was` sends a left shard what changed of its ghost of the near marker against the right base (taken, nothing asked) and a wrong one (STALE asked). When asked, it adds where its entities are (`load`, `sample`), and its checkpoint; having gone on from a checkpoint (`fresh`), its first report says what it owns and the ghosts it holds.
- It applies instructions (`TIDE_SHARD_INSTRUCTIONS`): messages to hand the server (an `ARRIVE` from a region marks its tree as sent toward the lender, which holds it as a ghost, and as `arriving`: its server's world hasn't got it until its next tick, so a report before that tick, which updates at twice the tick rate always had, dropped the lender's ghost, and the entity was in neither world for a tick; `arriving` keeps it from the drops until nothing handed is waiting), players to admit, its ghost targets (`DO_TARGETS`: shard, whether a region, box), a sample request, ghosts of its own other shards hold (`DO_HELD`, after a recovery), and a checkpoint (`DO_CHECKPOINT`), taken of the world as the batch finds it, before its messages are handed over.
- **The coordinator** (`engine/src/shard_coordinator.c`) touches no world. From reports it carries messages (`carry`: `TO_SHARD` by `live_shard`, `TO_OWNER` by the directory or the range an index is in, `TO_ALL`; an `ARRIVE` updates the directory), routes ghost messages, acknowledges (`acknowledge`, only when `drained`: every shard applied its last batch and has nothing handed waiting), follows the players (`track_players`, `route_players`), gives ranges, splits and merges (`reshape`), and makes checkpoints. Its decisions are per-shard instructions and actions for the host (`TIDE_SHARD_ACTION_START`, from a CONFIG at a tick or from a world and journal tick; `_STOP`; `_CHECKPOINT`, which the host keeps and confirms with `tide_shard_coordinator_kept`).
- **The root's region.** A world split into one region (`n[0]*n[1]*n[2] == 1`) is the root alone, its region the whole world (an infinite box): a hosted world, a dedicated server without `--shards`, a session's world. The root targets every region for ghosts (`write_targets`), and its agent reports arrivals from any shard, the root's own included (`note_arrival`). Sample requests go out even when nothing else does, so a lone root is measured and splits.
- Ranges of 2^20 indices; a shard with less than half a range left gets another.
- Splitting: every 30 updates, a region whose shard owns more than `most_entities` is asked where its entities are; with that, it's cut across its longest side at the median, keeping both halves at least twice the range across, and the new shard starts at the root's tick.
- **Load** (`tide_shard_agent_busy`, `tide_shard_coordinator_desc.busiest`): a host says how busy each shard is, the share of the time its updates take; the agent reports it (after `injected`) and the coordinator smooths it (`BUSY_SMOOTHING` 0.05 a report) and trusts it after `BUSY_SETTLING` (120) reports since the region last changed. `too_much` splits a region by entities or by `busy > busiest`; `quiet_together` merges two only if both are a quarter of each limit set. `tide_shards` times each server's update over the time since the last (`busy`), unless `tide_shards_desc.busy` says (tests, hosts); `tide_session` and `tide_serve` don't set `busiest`, as their shards share one machine. Managed: `tide_managed_shard_busy`, and `busiest` is the coordinator description's last f32.
- Merging: two regions whose boxes make one box (`joins`: equal on every axis but one, where they touch) and whose shards together own a quarter of `most_entities` at most merge, one at a time: the lower id's region becomes both, and the other leaves the map and is told `RETIRE` (into the lower), which its world keeps (`tide_shard_state.into`, the id + 1): its handoff sends what it owns by position, and what has no position (roots of trees) to that shard. Once it owns nothing, has nothing waiting, lent or unacknowledged, and nothing handed to apply, it's stopped (`finish_retiring`). Its id is never given again (`gone`: receivers keep its messages' last number), and a message routed to it goes to the shard it merged into (`live_shard`), so an entity lent to it on the way isn't lost. When the last region left is quiet with the root (which then has no region: it split away), it merges into the root (`maybe_merge`, `merge_into(c, ROOT, last, box)`), whose region becomes the whole world: the world is one shard again, as it started.
- **A change of shape waits for its checkpoint.** With checkpoints on, a split or merge is decided and its new shard started, but the map (and a `RETIRE`) is told only once a checkpoint with the new shape is kept (`tell_after`, released by `kept`). So no shard's journal depends on a shape no checkpoint has. Without checkpoints it's told at once.
- **Checkpoints** (`SAVE_VERSION` 5): when drained, idle and due (`checkpoint_seconds`, or the shape or ranges changed), the coordinator captures its own part (ranges, directory, players: whether each is in the world and their cookie) and asks every shard for its world; once all are in, the checkpoint is an action. Acknowledgements wait while one is gathered or kept. Loading starts every shard from its world (and journal records, recovering), and its first report rebuilds the directory from what it owns (one outside its own range, or in none, is listed) and tells each shard which of its entities others hold as ghosts; it sends each shard its ranges, a retiring one its `RETIRE` and every shard the regions again, as orchestrator messages on their way were lost with the servers, and admits the players everywhere with their cookies. `next_index` is the checkpoint's: a range is given only when drained and the change checkpointed.
- **One shard started again** (`tide_shard_coordinator_restarted`, `tide_shards_restart` in one process, `tide_shard_checkpoint_world` to find its world in a checkpoint): its host failed, so it starts elsewhere from the last checkpoint and its journal records. What it was handed and hadn't applied went with it, so every shard reports its unacknowledged messages again (`DO_FROM_START`, `delivered` back to `acked`; receivers drop what they had) and its ghosts whole (`DO_TARGETS` marks every entry of what it sent as of unknown bytes, so all goes again and what's gone is still dropped); the shard is told the regions, its ranges, its retirement and the players again, every route is told again, and its first report rebuilds where everything is. Tested by `shards_restart_one_shard_where_it_was`: shard 1 is started again mid-run, at the tick and hash it had, and the world goes on to the same end.
- **Managed instances** (`platform/include/tide/managed_run.h`, for a game whose header defines `TIDE_GAME_SHARDED`): `tide_managed_shard_new` (from a CONFIG), `_stage`/`_replay`/`_begin` (from a checkpoint's world and its records), `_report`, `_apply`, and the coordinator's `tide_managed_coordinator_create`/`_load`/`_report`/`_step`/`_actions` (as bytes)/`_instructions`/`_kept`/`_restarted`/`_checkpointing`/`_count`/`_set_match`, with `tide_managed_checkpoint_world` and `tide_managed_checkpoint_ticks` (each shard's tick in a checkpoint: its journal's records from before it can go); bytes given the host are in `tide_managed_bytes()`. The shard's journal goes to the host through the import `tide_host.journal`, which only a sharded game's module imports, so other games' modules are as they were.
- **In one process** (`engine/src/shards.c`), `tide_shards_update` updates every server, flushes the journal, then does a round: every agent reports, the coordinator steps, its actions are done (servers started from a CONFIG or a world, replaying records when recovering; stopped; checkpoints handed to `tide_shards_desc.checkpoint` and confirmed at once) and every agent applies its instructions. A checkpoint begun is gathered in another round within the same update. `tide_shards_save` packs every world at once (`tide_shard_coordinator_save`).

## Hot reload (`tide_session_can_reload`, `tide_shards_quiet`, `tide_shards_rebuilt`)

- A sharded match changes build at a quiet moment: no shard has `waiting` (a parked delivery or destroy, whose bytes are the old build's and whose event is chosen by a code that shifts with the systems), no `ARRIVE`, `EVENT` or `SINGLETON` is still handed to a server, and no checkpoint is being gathered. Between frames every outbox message has been reported and carried, so that's all there is. In `shards_test`'s busy world 86% of updates are quiet and the longest stretch without one is 16 updates.
- `tide_session_set_game` sets every shard's server's game and every followed client's, and `tide_shards_rebuilt`; `tide_session_migrate` of a sharded session prepares every shard's server and every followed client (one of this machine's shards takes its server's world, `take_server_world`; another machine's carries its own over), then commits them all or none. `tide_shards_rebuilt` drops `GHOSTS` still handed (`tide_server_drop_injected`), has every agent send its ghosts whole again (`tide_shard_agent_rebuilt`), and with a new layout asks for a checkpoint soon (`tide_shard_coordinator_rebuilt`).
- The native host (`reload_library.inc`) keeps a loaded build waiting (`reload.waiting`) and tries it every frame (`take_waiting`); after 3 seconds it swaps it in by starting over (`swap(..., unsettled)`), saying why. A newer build replaces a waiting one; a restart takes it.
- The web page asks the old program `tide_reload_ready` until it is, 3 seconds at most; `tide_reload_save` hands the match over as a checkpoint (`tide_session_save_shards`) and this machine's player's cookie, and the new program carries each shard's world over in it (`tide_shard_checkpoint_rewrite` with `tide_migrate_world`) and starts from it (`tide_session_start_shards_from`, which runs one orchestrator round first so the shards take their players back before this machine's client says hello).

## Physics (`packages/box3d`, `packages/box2d`; `notes/box3d.md`, `notes/box2d.md`)

- `Physics`' internal state is `[Native]` (its maps, lists, Box3D's resident state, Box2D's image, counters) and its settings the world's; `Body.position` is a `[Position]`, which decides where a body is. Handles in components are `[Native]` (`Body.id`, `settingsApplied`; shapes' `cache`; `Part`'s shapes and body; `Joint.id`, Box2D's `configuration`), so an arrival has none and is made anew from its `Body`.
- `Physics.shardMoves` follows `tide_shard_moves_now()`, read in C: the C topology check (`b3t_topology`, `b2t_topology`) calls a change in it a change of topology, so `Begin` looks over what each body's entity is, dropping bodies (and Box2D's fixtures, and sensors) whose entity this shard doesn't own any more.
- Copies: `CopyBodies`, a system for no entity with a query of every `Body`, makes or moves a body of its type for each one `Shards.Owns` says isn't this shard's (a dynamic one dynamic, with the body's gravity scale and dampings, as heavy as its shapes make it: until 2026-10-23 every copy was kinematic, infinite mass, so a crate running into one across the border stopped dead while the other left as fast as it came; `b3t_copy`/`b2t_copy` put it back each tick, and what's done to it in a step is no one's), with every shape it has (`CopyShapes`: Box3D's box, sphere, capsule, mesh, hull and height field; Box2D's box, circle, capsule, polygon, segment and chain), kept in `Physics.copies` (by entity, with the tick it was last seen) and `copyOf`; `CopyParts` (a query of every `Part`) puts each ghost part's shapes on its body's copy (`copiedParts`); `DropCopies` drops the ones not seen last tick or owned here now. `b3t_copy`/`b2t_copy` leave a copy at rest alone. `Report` names a copy's entity (`BodyOwner`) and sends events only to entities this shard owns.
- Settings are the root's on every shard: a game sets them as in any game.
- Newton (`notes/newton.md`, Sharded worlds): it keeps no native state between ticks, so its step's lists are `[Native]`, joints are `[Together]`, and `CollectNeighbours` puts neighbours' bodies in the step as they are, dynamic ones with their mass. Its contacts name no pairs, so it ties none: two bodies touching across a border stay on their own shards, each pushed by the other's lagging copy.
- Touching: `Report` (now with a `World`) says `Shards.Together(a, b)` as two dynamic bodies begin to touch (`Pushing`: what static or kinematic is pushes as its copy does, and tying a floor to everything on it would put a whole world on one shard) and `Shards.Apart(a, b)` for every touch that ends, so a pair that changed type meanwhile still balances. In codegen these are `tide_shard_pair_note` on the tick's runs (`tide_shard_tick.pairs`, under its lock, as systems run on threads; each run starts them over), and `tide_shard_touching` sums the last run's into `tide_shard_state.touching` (two keys, lower first, and a count) after sorting them, so the list is the same whatever order threads said them in; a pair goes at 0 or once neither entity is here. `tide_shard_ties_of` joins each pair, so the two go where their group's lead is, and its shard borrows the other. In a game that isn't sharded they compile to nothing; no world-hash pin moved.
- Joints: `Joint.a` and `Joint.b` are `[Together]`, so the joint and both its bodies are one group on one shard and it holds; `Part.of` is the first entity a part names, which it goes with. A joint whose bodies aren't both owned here (a moment, on the way) drops its native joint and waits (`SynchronizeJoints`).
- `Physics` grew (Box3D 352 bytes, Box2D 312; since, 376 and 336), so every world-hash pin of the packages, Sumo and Towers changed; bodies' and answers' pins didn't. `Body` is 176 and 96 bytes again since `heldBy` went with `[GoesWith]`, which moved the world and image pins again; the packages' system orders have `CopyBodies` and `CopyParts` in every game now (they do nothing in one that isn't sharded), and Box2D's `RemoveJoints` and an unordered game system swapped.

## Gaps

- A copy is where its owner last had it, a tick or two ago: two bodies racing toward each other meet a little late on one side and early on the other until they're on one shard. Each side reports only its own entities' touches.
- Bodies jointed together go together, so a long chain of jointed bodies is one shard's whatever its length.
- Visibility on regions lags the root's directory by a tick or two, so a group's narrowing shows a region's clients the old audience that long. Kept so (the owner, 2026-10-24): switching every shard at one tick only delays the root too. The spec says so.
- A game's code for no entity runs on the root, so its queries of a library's `[Native]` state (a raycast over the physics world from code for no entity) see the root's world only. Code for an entity sees its shard's.
- Host migration doesn't apply (shards are servers).
- The hosting service runs sharded worlds across machines (`Tide-Engine/tide-services`, its `notes/sharded-worlds.md`): its gaps (deploying hosts) are recorded there.
