## Rendering and platform

- **Audio is planned, and its eight cards are built** (2026-10-07; the design is `docs/spec.md`, Audio, with what's agreed and what's Claude's picks, and `docs/game/audio.md` is the users' page; the Tide side is whole but for what's listed as not built, a window plays through the system's device on every platform, clips are WAV or Ogg Vorbis, and the microphone is a clip). Audio is presentation, never the match: it reads the world and writes nothing to it, and the simulation never includes an audio header. What's built, and what code relies on:
  - **`tide/audio.h` is the frame's audio, as `tide/draw.h` is its drawing** (`engine/src/audio.c`): a `tide_audio_frame` records one-shots (`tide_audio_play`, `tide_audio_play_at`), voices named by an id (`tide_audio_voice`: clip, position, volume, pitch, loop, playing, bus, effect and its knobs, copied in), the listener, a falloff and each bus's volume and effect. `tide_audio_reset` empties it for a frame with every bus at 1 and nothing said, as `tide_draw_reset` does; every frame starts from the defaults. A `tide_clip` is interleaved float samples of any rate and 1 or 2 channels, which stay where they are while a voice plays them: the mixer reads them on its thread and copies nothing. A `tide_audio_effect` is code the audio thread runs once a block (`tide_audio_effect_fn(samples, state, knobs)`: 512 interleaved floats, the voice's own state of up to a megabyte, a copy of the frame's knobs of up to 256 bytes: `TIDE_AUDIO_STATE_BYTES`, `TIDE_AUDIO_KNOBS_BYTES`), named by an id whose change starts the state over; on a voice with no clip it's a source. The host records nothing into it yet: `tide_run_audio` in `tide/host.h` is reset each frame and handed over after the draw (`tide_platform_audio`), for the Tide side to fill when it comes.
  - **The mixer is `platform/src/mixer.c`**, stereo at 48 kHz in blocks of 256 frames (`TIDE_AUDIO_RATE`, `TIDE_AUDIO_BLOCK`, `TIDE_AUDIO_SAMPLES`), in floats, with nothing of a clock's: `tide_mixer_submit` (the frame's thread) copies a frame into one of three buffers and publishes it with one atomic exchange, and the renderer takes the newest at its next block (`tide_mixer_take`, from `tide_mixer_render`), so neither thread waits for the other; one-shots go through a ring of 1,024 (`TIDE_MIXER_ONE_SHOTS`), so none is lost between two blocks, and past it they're dropped and counted (`tide_mixer_dropped`). The renderer allocates nothing and blocks on nothing: 256 voice slots (`TIDE_MIXER_VOICES`, a new one past that taking the quietest's), each with its own knobs bytes and 4,096 of state (a bigger state is memory the frame's thread makes for it: A state can be big, below). `tide_mixer_render(out, frames)` renders any count by whole blocks, carrying the remainder, so any sizes give the same samples; `tide_mixer_flush` drops the remainder. What a block does: each voice's clip read at its own rate and pitch by linear interpolation (a double cursor), its effect, its gains ramped linearly over the block from the last block's (volume, and for a spatial voice equal-power pan by the listener's right and `near / distance` falloff scaled to 0 at `far`; defaults 1 and 50), into its bus; each bus's effect and ramped volume into the master; the master's out. A voice not named by a frame, or named with `playing` false, fades out over a block and goes; `playing` false then true, or another clip, starts the clip over; a named voice whose clip ended stays silent until then; a one-shot ends with its clip. A frame's `forget` stops everything at once and drops every effect, for a reload. Every float operation is a basic one (`__builtin_sqrtf` for the pan), so the mix is the same bytes on every platform: `tide_platform_audio` pins a hash of a scene of everything, the same natively and under Node.
  - **A headless run has audio with no device**: after each frame's function, the platform renders 800 frames (a 60th of a second) into a buffer that holds what a capture can still ask for and no more (`headless_audio_frame` in `platform/src/platform.c`: the whole run with `--capture-audio`, what played since the last `capture-audio` line while the script has another, `tide_script_captures_audio`, and otherwise a frame, so a long run that captures nothing stays flat; it grew by 12.5 MB a 30 seconds before 2026-10-07), `--capture-audio <file.wav>` (`tide_headless_desc.capture_audio`, `tide_run_configure`) writes the whole run as the run ends, and a script's `capture-audio <file.wav>` line (`EVENT_CAPTURE_AUDIO` in `platform/src/script.c`, `tide_platform_capture_audio`) writes what played since the last such line. The WAV writer is `platform/src/wav.c` (16-bit stereo PCM, samples clamped and rounded to the nearest, ties away from zero; `tide_wav_header` reads one back, for tests). A window with a device gets nothing from `tide_platform_audio` yet: the mixer is submitted frames and nothing renders them until the backends come (WASAPI, CoreAudio, ALSA at run time, AAudio, an `AudioWorklet`), each a device callback that calls `tide_mixer_render`.
  - **`Clip` and `Audio.Play` are the Tide side** (card 2). `Clip` is `TY_CLIP` (`tide_clip_ref`, 8 bytes: the name the pack's loader gives a declaration's sound, as a `Texture` of the pack's names its image; `tide_pack_clip_of`, `tide_pack_clip_find` in `engine/src/pack_load.c`, kinds `TIDE_PACK_CLIP` and `TIDE_PACK_CLIPS`, laid out after the region as textures are), an asset kind (`ASSET_CLIP`: `asset_files.c` decodes the file to check it, with the engine's own WAV reader, `tide/wav.h`, which tidec compiles too), and a resource of this machine's wherever a `Texture` is one (`holds_render_resource`, `element_ok` for an asset's glob only, the layout table in `codegen.c`). `tide_clip_of(ref)` (`engine/src/audio.c`) finds the file's bytes in the loaded pack and decodes them once, by what's in them, into a cache of the engine's own that `tide_clips_free` empties: so a voice never plays samples of a pack that's gone (a repack keeps the old file's beside the new), and a library's unload frees them after the host had the mixer forget (`tide_platform_audio_forget` before `old->unload()` in `platform/src/reload.c`: a reload stops every sound). `Audio` is a builtin owner (`builtins.c`: three `Play` signatures, plain, at a `float2`, at a `float3`, with volume and pitch defaults that codegen fills in as `1.0f`), its call `CALL_AUDIO`, checked by `check_audio_use` (views, local handlers, functions; the match, the input, tasks, methods and GPU code refused) and `check_audio_calls` (a function that plays is local code's to call, as `Frame.dt`'s is), and generated as `c_callee(tide_l->tide_audio, tide_clip_of(clip), ...)`: a function that plays takes the local state (`takes_local`), the local state has the frame's audio list when the game plays (`program.uses_audio`: `TIDE_HAS_AUDIO`, `tide_local.tide_audio`, `tide_local_audio`, which `tide_host_game.audio` is and the host calls before each frame). A game that plays nothing and has no `Clip` asset generates the C it did, byte for byte (old and new tidec over every game's source list, with `--layout` and without: 248 games the same, and the two that play, the e2e test and the capture game, only compile with the new one).
  - **`Sound` fields and the listener are card 3.** `Sound` is a built-in struct (`prog->sound` in `check.c`'s `add_builtins`, beside `Shape`; `named_only`, so it's in generated C, its blend helper and the layout only for a game that names it, which also sets `program.uses_audio`). The frame names every `Sound` of local state that plays: `gen_sounds` (`codegen.c`) writes `tide_local_sounds`, which walks local singletons, local archetypes' rows and the attached local parts of match components (the walk `gen_rebind_resources` makes, for `sound_fields`), structs in them too, and records each playing one as a `tide_voice` (`tide_sound_voice`: its clip through `tide_clip_of`) named by its field's path hashed and, for a row, the entity's bits (`tide_sound_entity`); `tide_frame` calls it last, after the local commands applied. `Audio.SetListener` (a `float3` and a quaternion, or a `float2`) and `Audio.SetFalloff` are `Audio`'s other signatures, through `CALL_AUDIO` like `Play` (whose default-filling in `gen_builtin_call` is by name, `Play` alone). Generated C for a game that names no `Sound` is byte for byte what it was (old and new tidec over every game's source list, with `--layout` and without: 252 games the same, and the `sound` e2e test only compiles with the new one).
  - **Buses and `audio` declarations are cards 4 and 5.** `[Bus]` marks the game's enum of buses (`prog->bus_enum`, found right after `collect_decls` so `Sound.bus` resolves as it, checked in `check_attributes`); `Audio.SetVolume` and `Audio.SetEffect` are checked by hand (`check_audio_volume`, `check_audio_bus_effect`: the bus as the enum, or a number with none) and generated with a `(uint32_t)` cast. An `audio` declaration is a `DECL_FUNCTION` with `is_audio` (`parse_audio`; `check_audio_signature` sorts its parameters into `audio_state` and `audio_knobs`; `check_audio_bodies` refuses what the audio thread can't do, after the call-site passes, with `makes_text` noted per function in `check_expr` and propagated through calls); a call of one is `CALL_EFFECT`, an `Effect` value (`check_effect_value`), generated as `tide_effect_<Name>(knobs)`. `gen_effects` (in `gen_sounds`) writes each declaration's wrapper `tide_audio_fn_<Name>` (the block, state and knobs cast, with `0u` for each `mut` parameter's `where`), the `tide_effects` table by id and `tide_effect_of`, which the voice walk and `tide_audio_set_bus_effect_value` resolve an `Effect` with; `TIDE_AUDIO_KNOBS_BYTES` is 256, what an `Effect` holds. **Three built-in structs are in generated C only for a game that names them** (`builtin_struct_unnamed`: `Sound`, `Samples`, `Effect`), and so are the arrays only their fields made (`array_unwritten`, with `order_arrays` numbering them after the game's own); naming `Sound` names `Effect`, and `program.quiet_names` keeps a built-in struct's own field resolution from counting as the game naming anything. Old and new tidec over every game's source list, with `--layout` and without: every game that plays no sound the same, byte for byte.
  - **An `audio` declaration's state and knobs have a size, which is an error past it** (2026-10-08; part of what No Tide code can corrupt memory rests on: see Language). Until then a state was the 4,096 bytes every voice had, the generated wrapper cast them to the game's struct and `engine/src/audio.c` only clamped `state_size`, so a state with an `int[4096]` in it (16 KiB) was written into the next voice's slot, and knobs over 256 bytes were a `_Static_assert` of generated C's, an error with none of tidec's words. What's built, and what code relies on:
    - `check_audio_sizes` (`compiler/src/check.c`, right after `check_audio_bodies`, so once every call is known) is an error at the state parameter for a struct over `TIDE_AUDIO_STATE_BYTES` (1,048,576 since big states, below: "an audio declaration's state is 1048576 bytes at the most, and 'Tail' is 1048580") and at the knobs parameter for one over `TIDE_AUDIO_KNOBS_BYTES` (256), each with a note that says to make the struct smaller. A declaration that rolls (`decl.rolls`, which is why the check comes after `check_roll_calls`) has a `Random`'s 8 bytes less, as its wrapper keeps its generator after its state: the error says "where it rolls with Random", and its note where it rolls. check.c reads both numbers from `tide/audio.h`, so a limit that changes there changes the error.
    - **A type's size is `types.h`'s, for the checker and codegen alike**: `type_layout`, `decl_layout` and `decl_layout_padded` are in `compiler/src/types.c` (they were codegen's own), and codegen's `field_padding` gets the padding it writes out from there. So the checker's number is the one generated C asserts of each struct it writes (`_Static_assert(sizeof(...) == ...)`), and editors show the error, which a check made in codegen wouldn't. A type whose bytes change changes them there, once. Moving it changed no game's generated C (old and new tidec over every game's source list, with `--layout` and without: 225 games).
    - **A size that's more than an int says is `LAYOUT_MOST`** (`saturated` in `types.c`), never what the int wraps to: structs of arrays of structs say gigabytes in a few lines, four of them wrapped to 0, and 0 fits anything. The error then says "over 2147483647". Sizes under that are what they were.
    - Tests: `compiler/tests/errors/audio_state_too_big`, `audio_state_too_big_structs` (a struct's padding counts), `audio_state_too_big_rolls` (four bytes short of the most, and a roll through a function), `audio_state_too_big_wraps` and `audio_knobs_too_big`; `lsp_audio_state_and_knobs_sizes` for the editors.
  - **A state can be big: an echo's or a reverb's delay line** (2026-10-08. The owner: "i do want echo or reverb to be a future feature so maybe we prepare now", then, asked, that a long delay line is written as a longer array in the state, and that it's built now. The megabyte, the 4,096 bytes a voice keeps itself and every rule here are Claude's picks, Provisional in `docs/spec.md`, Audio). `docs/game/audio.md` (Echoes and reverbs) is the users' page. What's built, and what code relies on:
    - **`TIDE_AUDIO_STATE_BYTES` is 1,048,576**, the most a state takes (2.7 seconds of stereo), and `TIDE_AUDIO_STATE_SMALL` 4,096, what a voice and a bus keep in themselves, as every state was (`tide/audio.h`). The frame records no effect for one that says more than the most (`copy_knobs` in `engine/src/audio.c`, which clamped it: its code would write past what it's given).
    - **A state over `TIDE_AUDIO_STATE_SMALL` is memory of its own, which the frame's thread makes and frees** (`big_state`, `state_for`, `free_settled` in `platform/src/mixer.c`), as the renderer allocates nothing. `tide_mixer_submit` keeps one for each voice id (or bus) and effect (id and size) its frames play, zeros as it's made, and hands it with every frame that plays it (`handed.states`, beside `handed.voices`, and `bus_state`); a voice's slot points at it (`voice.big`, `bus.big`), and the effect runs in it. With no state to run in, an effect isn't run: a one-shot's, which has none.
    - **The frame's thread decides when a big state starts over**: a frame that doesn't play the voice (not named, or `playing` false), or that gives it another effect, lets the state go, and the next that plays it gets another, zeros. The renderer goes by which state it's handed (`update_voice`: another one is the effect started over; a voice that stops keeps the one it had through its fade). So where two frames come between two blocks and the first doesn't play the voice, a big state starts over though the renderer never saw the voice stop, where a small one, which is its slot's, goes on. A stolen slot's voice gets its state back in its next slot.
    - **Freeing waits for the renderer, with no lock and nothing timed.** A frame has a serial (`handed.serial`); the renderer says the last frame it applied and rendered a block after (`m.settled`, stored at the end of `render_block`, and as a forget is applied): by then every voice that frame stopped has faded and gone, and every other has that frame's states. A state retired at frame S (the first that doesn't hand it) is freed at the first submit that finds `settled` at S or past it. **A state the renderer never had is freed at once**: publishing a frame gets back what was pending, which says whether the renderer took the frame before (`PENDING_NEW` still set: it didn't, and now can't), so each state knows whether any frame the renderer took handed it (`seen`). So a machine with no device, where `tide_platform_audio` submits and nothing renders, holds what plays and nothing more, and a device that stalled holds at most what it had as it stalled. `tide_mixer_forget` frees everything once the forget is applied. `tide_mixer_states()` counts them, for tests.
    - **The pinned mix is what it was** (`721523a2f7eebcb3`): states up to 4,096 bytes go through the code they did.
    - **A long array is one of more than `TIDE_ARRAY_MOST` (4,096) elements**, up to `TIDE_ARRAY_LONGEST` (as many as a state has bytes), all of its lengths together: `float[96000] line;`. `array_type` (`compiler/src/check.c`) makes it anywhere a type is written, and where it can be is checked once types are known. `type_holds_long_array` (`types.c`, with `TIDE_ARRAY_MOST`: codegen asks too) says whether a type is one or holds one through arrays and structs (a struct remembers: `decl.long_arrays`), and `reaches_long_array` also through what a list, a map, a set, a grid or a `T?` holds. Asked only once `checker.fields_ordered`: before that a struct can still hold itself.
    - **A value with a long array in it is only ever read where it is, and one gate says so**: `check_expr_any` refuses an expression of such a type ("'Tail' is never copied...") unless what's checking it said it reads it in place (`checker.in_place_ok`, the pattern `device_whole_ok` and `colors_indexed` are): a member's object (`check_member`), an index's object (`check_index`), and an argument a `mut` parameter takes (`check_method_args`; `check_overloaded_args` lets every argument through and says once the function is picked). So a local of one, an assignment either way, a struct's literal, `default`, a `foreach`'s source, `==`, text and a by-value argument are all that one error, with no list of places to keep up. **A new way to read a value in place sets `in_place_ok`; a new way to use a value needs nothing.**
    - **Where the type can't be written** is `resolve_type` (a local, a loop's variable, a constant, what a function returns, a container's element once fields are ordered) and `check_long_arrays`, after every signature: fields of anything but a struct, containers in fields (resolved before fields are ordered), parameters that aren't `mut` (and any parameter of a shader, an operator, an `extern` or an async function), returns, and a struct's methods (`this` is a copy). A long array itself in the wrong place is the error an array that's too long always was ("an array holds from 1 to 4096 elements..."), with a note that says where a longer one goes.
    - **Generated C never has one by value, and can't**: an array read where the array is already went through `tide_array<n>_ref` (`pointerable`, `gen_ptr`), writes through `_set` and `_slot`, and a `mut` parameter is a pointer. `gen_array_helpers` writes no `tide_array<n>_get`, the reader that takes its array by value, for a type that holds a long array, so code that would copy one doesn't compile. `tidec_e2e_effects` also builds with `-Wframe-larger-than=65536`: a function of generated C with such a copy on its stack is an error there. A `foreach` over an array copies it first (`gen_stmt`), which is why a long one is refused. Evaluation order's temporaries (`tide_read<n>`) are only made of values that are taken, never of a member's or an index's object, which is a place.
    - **A rolling declaration's generator is after its own state, on the next 8 bytes** (`rolled_at` in `codegen.c`), and its `state_size` is both, where it was the last 8 bytes of the 4,096 and all of them: a megabyte for every voice that rolls otherwise.
    - **Generated C** differs for the games with an `audio` declaration that rolls (the generator's place, and its state's size), and for no other: old and new tidec over every game's source list, with `--layout` and without.
    - **Not built:** a `Sound`'s share sent to another bus (what lets one reverb on a bus serve many sounds, each at its own amount: today a bus's effect is on all of the bus); an array's element that holds a long array passed on as `mut` (the function takes what holds the array, and which element); a state of a size the game picks as it runs; reading a state back from a view; the stats' line for what states take.
    - **Tests:** `tide_platform_audio` (C effects through the mixer: an echo whose line is a second of stereo, kept between blocks, a second voice's own, started over when its voice isn't named, stops and starts, or changes code, freed a block after; a bus's; frames nobody renders, which hold nothing once their voices stop; two frames between two blocks; the sizes at each edge, 4,096 and a byte more, the most and a byte more; a one-shot); `tidec_e2e_effects` (an echo written in Tide with `float[96000]`, called as the mixer calls it between guard bytes: a block again 512 frames later at half, around the line's end, indexes past both ends, a function it's passed on to, and one that rolls beside a long state); `tide_platform_capture` (label `gpu`: the same echo in a run with no window, through the mixer, read off the WAV at the frame, stopped before its echo and started again, the same bytes twice); `compiler/tests/safety/audio.tide`, whose states are the most there can be, a megabyte, written end to end and indexed anywhere, one of them a rolling declaration's with its generator after it (`safety/host.c`'s `run_effect` gives each effect the bytes it says it keeps between guard bytes it checks after each block; with the generator's 8 bytes left out of what an effect says, it fails, "an effect wrote outside its state": tried); `compiler/tests/errors/long_array_*.tide`, a file a way to misuse one; `lsp_audio_state_and_knobs_sizes`; and `tide_platform_audio_device`, where the machine has a device: voices and a bus with states of 200 KB come and go for 60 frames while the device's own thread renders, each state made on the test's thread, written all over on the device's (which checks every byte is what its last block left, or zeros) and freed by the test's, with none left after the forget.   - **The device is card 6.** `platform/src/audio_out.h` is what each build has of it: `tide_audio_out_open` (with the window, in `tide_platform_open`; a run with no window opens none, and neither does a window in a run nobody watches, `TIDE_HIDDEN`, which opens no microphone either: `notes/standard-host.md`, Nobody listens either) and `_close` (in `finish`), and `tide_audio_out_fill`, the one pull every backend fills its device from (`audio_out.c`: the mixer a block at a time, read out at the device's rate by linear interpolation between two of the mixer's frames, a mono device averaged, more channels given the two and zeros; one thread at a time, the device's). A backend that renders on a thread of its own says so (`tide_mixer_rendered_elsewhere`), and `tide_mixer_forget` then waits for that thread to take the forget (`forget_asked`, `forget_done`), half a second at most. **It says so before that thread can ask for a block, which is also what makes the mixer** (`tide_mixer_init`, lazy, from whichever call comes first): WASAPI's said it after its thread had started the device, alone of the four, so on Windows the device's first block could come while the frame's thread was inside `tide_mixer_init`, which had set `made` before it allocated the voices, and the device's thread rendered from none (a null read at `render_block`'s first voice). Written on Windows, where none of it compiles: neither built nor run until CI's next run.
  - **Ogg Vorbis is card 7** (`tide/vorbis.h`, `engine/src/vorbis.c`: the engine's own decoder from the Vorbis I specification and RFC 3533, which tidec compiles too for the build's check; floats, with a sine of its own, so a file is the same samples everywhere; floor type 0 and more than two channels refused). `tide_vorbis_open` reads every page (checksums checked) and copies the packets out, parses the headers and decodes nothing; a `tide_vorbis_reader` decodes from the start, as many frames as asked, and rewinds; `tide_vorbis_decode` is the whole file as `tide_wav_decode` gives one. The reader stops at the last page's granule. A clip's decoding moved to load: `tide_clips_prepare(jobs)` (called by `tide_run_assets_from` in `tide/host.h`, so at start and at each pack `tide run` writes) decodes every clip of the pack the cache hasn't got, each file once, on the pool's threads (`tide_pack_clips_each` lists them), a WAV whole and Vorbis whole up to `tide_clips_whole_seconds` (10); a longer Vorbis clip is kept opened, and its `tide_clip` says `stream` with no `samples`. The mixer streams such a clip (`read_stream` in `platform/src/mixer.c`): the voice's `reader` is made at its first block, two frames (`a`, `b`) go through the same interpolation a decoded clip's samples do, a loop rewinds at the end, and `clear_voice` closes the reader wherever a slot is emptied (a forget among them), before `tide_clips_free` closes the files.
