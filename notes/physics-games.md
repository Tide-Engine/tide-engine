# The physics games: Towers and Sumo

Build notes, moved here from `AGENTS.md` as they were: what's built, what code relies on, the tests and the measurements. `AGENTS.md` has the rules every session needs and the index of these files. The headings below are that file's sections as they were, and "see <Section>" names one of them: the index says which files each is in. What a note calls "below" or "above" may be in another of these files now: search `notes/` for its name.

## Building

### The physics games

Two small games the owner asked for (2026-10-06), to show the physics packages in a match and to play with other people on other machines: Towers (`examples/towers/`, on Box2D, four players) and Sumo (`examples/sumo/`, on Box3D, eight). Towers took the place of Siege, the first Box2D game (two towers of crates and four cannons), the same day: the owner found the two games' play "a bit weird" and "a bit messy", wanted them to "make more sense" and to be "more massive in terms of physics", and picked Tricky Towers for 2D. They were toys of two files each; the owner then asked for one instance with a lobby, and polish, and for their pages to stay where they are in the sidebar (in Get started, after Duel). Their names, their rules, bots in Towers' empty seats, the lobby's pages and that players drop in with no ready gate are Claude's picks, which the owner hasn't confirmed.

Sumo took the place of Alley, a bowling game, the same day. The owner found the two games' play "a bit weird" and "similar", wanted them to "make more sense" and to be "more massive in terms of physics, like push physics more, better, higher", and picked "Smash arena, a sumo type game" for the 3D one. That a player is a heavy ball they drive, in a ring packed with physics, and shoves the others out of it is agreed. Its name, its rules (the charge, the bump and the daze, who a ring-out scores for, the rims, two minutes a round), its pickups and kegs, its bots, its camera, and that rubble breaks up are Claude's picks, which the owner hasn't confirmed.

- **Each is a folder as `tide run` builds it:** `game.tide` is the match; `play.tide` the camera and the pieces; `effects.tide` the sparks and the numbers; `hud.tide` the scores, the words and the buttons; Towers' `bots.tide` how a tower nobody steers is built; Sumo's `light.tide` its meshes and frags, and its `arena.tide` what a round's ring is built of, which is the match's too; and `tide.packages` says `box2d` or `box3d`, and `rooms`. Views say their layer (`[Stage(Layers.<x>)]`), as tide compiles a folder's files in the order of their names, which isn't the order they draw in. `tide_add_game` reads no `tide.packages`, so each one's `CMakeLists.txt` lists the files (in that order) and names its packages as `docs/examples/CMakeLists.txt` does (each package's Tide file after its name in `PACKAGES`, Box2D's or Box3D's `bridge.c` with the game's C, and `tide_box2d` or `tide_box3d`), which is why the top `CMakeLists.txt` adds them after `packages`. Their tests are in `test/`, a folder with a `tide.packages` of its own: a game's folder compiles every `.c` in it, so a test beside the game's files would be part of the game under `tide run`, and a folder with that file is no part of the game around it (`folder_find_own`).
- **The lobby and the menu are the rooms package's** (since 2026-10-07; see Rooms, under Packaging and releases, for what it does and what a game reads of it). Each had a `lobby.tide` of its own before, the same in both, whose design the package keeps: local state over a live match, never a scene (`Main` stays the match's, so the standard host starts a match of this machine's own at once, closed, and Towers' four towers build by themselves behind the title; chosen over a local `Main` like Duel's because the host refuses `--host` there, and because the title has a game running behind it for free), a machine told which match to be in with no page in its way, `Disconnected` putting a machine back on the title with a line that says why, and the player doing nothing while a page is up. What's each game's own, in `hud.tide`:
  - **`Hand.playing` is the package's `Lobby.playing`,** copied by the view that writes `Hand` (Towers' `Hands` in `play.tide`, Sumo's `Controls`), which the input's `Sample` reads (Towers' `Controls.hands`, whose match then builds the player's tower for them; Sumo's `Controls.away`, an `[Absolute]` field: a player whose page is up isn't left standing, their ball plays by itself as a bot's does, since a ball that stood still would be thrown out again and again). The HUD's views draw nothing while `lobby.page != Page.Playing`.
  - **`Roster`** tells the room's page who has each seat (`lobby.seats`, `manned`, `mine`, `bots`) and that the game has a new game and a bots' switch (`offersNewGame`, `offersBots`), and sends `Rebuild` and `Bots` in the frame the page's buttons say so (`lobby.newGame`, `toggleBots`). Play on the title sets `newGame` too, so a game of one's own starts from its start, as it did.
  - **Sumo's stick and its charge button aren't the GUI's:** a view reads them from the devices (`Controls`: every finger of the touchscreen, and the mouse), as a second finger has to hold one while the first holds the other, and the GUI follows one pointer. Towers keeps `Pill` (`GUI.Control`, drawn by the game) for the power's button. The `Menu` layer is gone from both `Layers` enums: the package's pages are modals of the GUI's, drawn over everything.
- **Seats.** Towers' four plinths are there from the start (`Towers.plinths`, by seat), and a player gets `Owner` on the first nobody has (`Towers.seats`): the join's handler goes by the singleton's map, never by a component of the plinth, as the plinths' spawn may be in the same tick's queue behind it. A seat with no player builds by itself (`bots.tide`, from `Deal`), which a command turns off, and so does the seat of a player whose lobby or menu is up (`Controls.hands`): the title has four towers building behind it, and a player who opens the menu comes back to a tower that went on. A falling brick has `Owner` too, which is whose input steers it. Sumo's eight `Seat`s are there from the start (`Ring.chairs`), and a player has the first that no player has (`Ring.manned`, a bit a seat, with `Ring.players` and `Ring.owners`). A seat that's in the match (`Live`: a player's, or one of the first that bots have until four balls are in the ring) has a ball, an entity of its own with the body, which comes and goes: `Seats` spawns it, `Roll` destroys it when it's under `OUT` and tells the seat (`Fell`), and it's back `RESPAWN` ticks later. `Hands` keeps the ball's `Owner` its seat's player, so a player who joins takes a bot's ball as it is. Every ball has a `Brain` (`Think`, with a query over the other balls), which `Drive` uses for a seat no player has and for a player whose input says `away`.
- **Towers' bricks.** Its rules are its page's (`docs/guide/towers.md`): steer the falling brick, be first to stand over the line for three seconds. What code relies on:
  - **A brick is one entity and one body from when it's dealt to the round's end** (since 2026-10-06, on what the package gained that day; before, it was a falling entity of sensors, then a dynamic one spawned in its place, then a kinematic one if it set). `towers_a_brick_is_steered_then_becomes_a_body` checks the entity and Box2D's handle stay the same.
  - **A falling brick is a kinematic body with no shapes** (`Falling`), which falls by its velocity and is put across and turned by having its position and angle written, from its owner's input. The input says where the player wants the brick and how turned, not which keys are held (`Controls.column` and `quarter`, both `[Absolute]`, worked out a frame at a time by the view `Hands` into the local `Hand`), and which brick that's about (`serial`: `Steer` only goes by an input that names the brick, so the next brick isn't sent where the last was).
  - **What's in its way is asked of the world** (the package's `Cast` and `Overlap`, through the game's `Free` and `Room`): `Steer`, before the step, turns the brick only where there's room for it turned, steps it half a cell only where it's free to, and lets it fall while it's free to go this tick's fall. A brick is never inside anything, so nothing is put back or undone. `Slim` makes each box `SLACK` smaller where it's asked about, as a cast shape touches what it's flush with: narrower only, for the way down, so that it lands flush.
  - **It lands where the cast says it touches**: its position is written there, at rest, its `type` becomes `Dynamic`, and its shapes go on, the first as its own `Box` (`this.Add`, with `Brick`) and the second, for the five bricks of two boxes, as a `Part` (`Spawn(Box, Part { of = this })`). `Fit` and its call into the package's `AttachBox` are gone, with the docs' warning. A brick lands at rest however fast it fell, which is what the two-touch landing was for: how fast a brick is when it becomes a body is what cost towers most (over 24 seeds, bots lost 25 bricks a tower a round landing at up to 5 m/s, 10 at 3, 4 at 1.5).
  - **Its view draws where it will land** (`Fallings` in `play.tide`): the same `Free`, 60 m down (`SIGHT`), asked each frame of the world the view draws, for this machine's player's brick: lanes down to an outline.
  - **Every brick is loose, from the plinth up**: nothing sets by itself (`SET_DEPTH` is gone). `Build` tunes the world for it: `substeps` 8, `contactStiffness` 60, `contactDamping` 40 (`SUBSTEPS`, `STIFFNESS`, `DAMPING`). How that was arrived at, bricks lost a tower a round by bots with no powers used: 11.0 with every brick loose at 60 and 8 with Box2D's damping of 10 (24 seeds), 7.4 at 120 and 16 substeps, then by damping at 60 and 8 over 48 seeds, 5.8 at 20, 4.5 at 40, 4.5 at 80, 6.7 at 160. The game before it, with the lower bricks solid, lost 4.8 (48 seeds), and 3.0 with the new tuning and bricks still set 10 m under the top: the towers are loose because the owner asked how much could be, not because it's steadier. `towers_a_tall_tower_is_loose_all_the_way_down` builds one to 24 m, checks no brick is set and that it's at rest a minute on with every brick where it was.
  - **Putting a tower's lower bricks to sleep isn't a way to steady it** (tried 2026-10-06, with a body's `asleep`, on the game as it was while its lower bricks were set solid, in the place of that): a tower is one island, its plinth with it, so the bricks low in it can't sleep while the ones over them are awake. Written `asleep` as they came to rest, they took the whole tower with them each time, the brick that had just landed among it, and every landing woke it all again: over 12 seeds of four bots, no tower lost a brick in 75 seconds, where they lost 8 with the bricks set, stood 35 m high where they stood 28, and no gust or quake did anything. That's no tower to bring down, and the game uses none of it.
  - **`Cement` is all that sets a brick** (`Brick.set`, `Towers.sets`): `Tend` writes the `type` of every brick at rest in the tower to `Kinematic` (through `world.Set<Body>(this, ...)`, as it reads every brick and writes a few), and `Push` gives each its plinth's velocity (`Towers.sways` and `rises`, which `Move` leaves), so a quake and a late joiner's rise carry it. When a round is over they're `Dynamic` again, from the bottom of each tower up (`CRUMBLE`), the winner's last, and everything comes down.
  - **A brick is off its tower** under `FALLEN`, or `SPILL` past its plinth's side, whatever it rests on: before the second rule, what fell between two plinths piled up to 20 m and counted as tower.
  - **A round ends.** From `LIMIT` ticks in, the line (`Towers.line`) comes down to the towers, to `LOWEST` at the least: without it, sabotage kept four bots under the line for five minutes.
  - **`bots.tide` plans from six column heights**: how far from level the top is left (squared, so the lowest columns are filled first), the room under the brick, the steps along the top, a brick whose middle would be over nothing, and one that would rest on a brick askew. A cell counts as resting on its column within 0.35 m: at 0.08, a top that wasn't level to the centimeter made every wide brick look unsupported, and the bots built spires one cell wide. Each seat is a little more or less careful, and quick, for a round (`temper`), so no two towers are alike.
  - **What the towers do to each other:** a gust is an impulse a tick, for `GUST` ticks, on the loose bricks within `WIND_DEPTH` of the top of the tallest other tower (by each brick's highest corner, not its body's place, which is under that for a brick that stands upright); a quake is every other plinth going there and back as a sine of its velocity, with what stands on it. A quake reaches all of a loose tower, where it used to carry the solid part: with every earned power made a quake, towers lost 28.9 bricks a round where the old game's lost 10.8, so `SHAKE` went from 1.2 to 0.9; `WIND` stayed 1.3. Over 48 seeds with every power in play, the game loses 16.9 bricks a tower a round, in rounds of 89 seconds (18.4 with `SHAKE` at 1.2); before, 16.1 in rounds of 98. With none used, 4.5 against 4.8.
- **Scores are by who touched last,** in Sumo: a ball remembers the seat whose ball touched it (`Ball.last`, for `BLAME` ticks; a blast counts as its owner's touch, `Rocked`), and when it goes over the rim that seat is sent `Scored`. A round is two minutes, and it's whoever threw out the most, the first to get there if two did (`Seat.round`, `Seat.scored`). Views read scores `verified`. Towers has no score but how high each tower stands (`Seat.top`, the highest corner of the bricks at rest over its plinth, which `Measure` reads with a query) and the rounds a seat has taken (`Seat.wins`).
- **What a machine shows comes from events the match sends for it,** which nothing in the match handles, so they cost it nothing: Towers' `Landed` (sent to the brick that landed, which is how its handler lights it), `Lost`, `Earned`, `Struck`, `RoundOver` and `RoundBuilt`, and the Box2D package's `Hit` (pieces fly where bricks meet faster than `CRASH`, the world's `hitSpeed`), Sumo's `RingOut`, `Clash`, `Smash`, `Burst`, `Boom`, `Taken`, `Dropped`, `Shrinking`, `Go`, `RoundOver` and `RoundSet`. Local handlers (`effects.tide`) make sparks, debris, a camera shake and the numbers that float up of them; a score's number and a round's winner are `verified` handlers'. The events carry what their handlers show (where, whose, how much), so no handler takes `Towers` or `Ring`: it could, but not name the lists and maps they hold, which aren't heard. What fades or eases (a landed brick's light, a falling brick's slide across and its swing round) is local fields.
- **Sumo's look** (`light.tide`, `play.tide`): every mesh's corners have a normal, and one function (`Lit`) lights a pixel for every frag. Nothing is drawn an entity at a time but the balls: a view for each crate adds a `Solid` to a list (`Fx.solids`), and one `Draw.Instanced` draws the list, so a frame of 2,400 crates is under thirty draw calls. Shadows are a shadow map (the next point). The floor is a `Grid2<Color>` of 15 by 15 in a local singleton (`Ground.floor`: there, or about to go), which the tiles' view writes each frame and the frags sample as a texture, for the line of light along an edge with no tile past it and for where what's flat on the floor stops. It's a singleton of its own because one with a grid can't be passed to a function a frag calls (`Room`, which `Lit` takes, has only floats), and the frags sample it themselves because a function can't (see `FloorPaint`'s parameters). Marks on the floor (the rings under the balls, a blast's edge) are one list of `Decal`s at one height (`ON_FLOOR`), drawn inside `Draw.Depth(Depth.Read)`, with no light of the ring's on them: they're the same in a shadow. The sparks, which glow and add both of a cube's sides, are inside `Draw.Cull(Cull.None)`; everything else is drawn from its front, so every mesh `light.tide` makes is wound clockwise as it's seen from outside, the floor's plate from above. The camera always looks from the same side, so the stick's up is the same way in the ring for everyone: it shows the whole ring when a ball is still 20 pixels across at that size, and as much as that takes otherwise, around this machine's ball and towards the middle, or around the middle while that ball is out (`Look`). A ball's own mark (`Tags` in `hud.tide`) is drawn in the screen's pixels, over everything, as towers hide balls.
- **Sumo's shadows are a shadow map** (2026-10-07, from the raw pieces the owner asked for where a shadows feature might have been: a target of depth alone, and a frag that compares with it; see Rendering and platform, A target says what it holds). Until then its casters were drawn a second time inside `Draw.Transform(Flatten())`, a matrix that slid every point along the lamp's light onto the floor, through a frag that painted the floor again there, darker: shadows fell on the floor and on nothing else. That it's a shadow map is the owner's; every choice here is Claude's, which the owner hasn't confirmed. What's built, and what code relies on:
  - **The lamp's image** is `Shadow.map`, `RenderTarget(LAMP_PIXELS, LAMP_PIXELS, 1, Format.Depth)`, which the view `Cast` makes the first time it runs (`Shadow.made`: nothing says whether a target was made), with `Shadow.depth`, its `DepthTexture`, and `Shadow.toLamp`, the world to the lamp's clip space, which `Cast` reads each frame from the target's own camera (`shadow.map.WorldToClip()`, since the cameras are read as matrices; it spelled the turn of z out itself before). `Shadow` is a singleton of its own that each frag takes itself: a singleton with a target in it can't be passed to a function a frag calls ("Shadow can't go to the GPU"). So the four frags that light something (`TileLook`, `CrateLook`, `BallLook`, `Plain`) each hand its depth and its matrix to one function, `Sunlight(shadow.depth, world, normal, shadow.toLamp)`, which works out where the place is in the lamp's image (`FromLamp`) and gives the mean of four `Compare`s there, `LAMP_SOFT` apart, which the frag hands `Lit` (`sun`). Until 2026-10-08 a function couldn't read a depth it was passed, and each of the four had those four `Compare`s itself; the picture is the same bytes (`notes/shaders.md`, A function samples the textures it takes).
  - **`Cast` is at `Layers.Lamp`, before anything draws to the window,** whose `Draw.Clear` moved from `Look` to `Floor` to be with the window's other draws: a frame's list starts with the lamp's target, which `sumo_effects_come_from_what_the_match_sends` checks. That's still three passes as the renderer is, one of them empty, and not two: `draw_list` (`platform/src/platform.c`) begins the window's pass as a frame starts, whatever the list's first command is, ends it at the lamp's target with nothing drawn, and begins it again after, with what was there. A renderer that began the window's pass at the first command for it would make such a frame two; nothing here measured what the empty one costs (on a desktop GPU the whole frame is 0.1 ms), and it's reported to the owner, not changed.
  - **What casts is the lists the window is drawn from,** with no frag and no vert, so an instance sends its transform and nothing else: `fx.tiles`, `fx.solids` and `fx.gems` with the box, which are one run as they're drawn in a row (2,458 instances with a ring standing), then `fx.rounds` and `fx.orbs` with the ball. With the clear that's 3 of a frame's draw calls, where the flattened shadows were 2. Pickups cast too, so they're gathered into lists (`Beacons`) that `Pickups` draws, where each was a draw of its own. The tiles cast, so what falls past the far rim goes into the floor's shadow. `Solid.low`, `Flatten`, `ShadowDrop` and `ShadowLook` are gone. The lamp's pass has a target's first cull: what's stored is each caster's side towards the lamp.
  - **The image is fixed to the ring as a round starts:** a box 40 m across (`LAMP_SPAN`: the floor is 39.1 m across and 31.2 up the image, where a stack 13 m high leans 7.8 more), its middle 2.9 m up the image from the ring's (`LAMP_RAISED`), seen from 60 m off along the light with no perspective. It doesn't follow the ring as the ring shrinks, on purpose: a box that changed size would slide its pixels over the ring, and every shadow's edge would crawl. As it is, a standing crate's shadow is the same pixels every frame.
  - **1,024 pixels a side** is 3.9 cm of the ring a pixel: half a pixel of the docs' frame (375 wide, where the camera shows 30 m, 8 cm a pixel), one at a display scale of 2, one and a half at 3, and about one in a window of 1080 lines that shows the whole ring (4.6 cm). Looked at beside 512, which blurs a crate's shadow at a scale of 3, and 2,048, which is sharper there and takes 0.03 ms more of the GPU here and up to 0.05 (0.08 to 0.11 at the docs' frame, 0.12 to between 0.15 and 0.17 at a scale of 3), and 16 MB where this takes 4.
  - **The bias is off the surface, not nearer the lamp** (`FromLamp`): a pixel is looked up from `normal * step * (0.4 + 1.8 * slant)` off its place, `step` a pixel of the image and `slant` the sine of the angle between its normal and the lamp, which is as far as four looks 0.75 of a pixel apart reach, each blended over the pixels around it. Tried, at the docs' frame with a display scale of 3: with none of it everything lit is striped; with half of it tumbling crates have a moiré (44,653 pixels of 1.35 million more than 8 of 255 off); a depth bias of a centimeter on top changed 206 of them, at shadows' edges, and is left out. A pixel of the floor beside a crate is lifted, and still behind the crate's side, so a shadow starts at its crate.
  - **A pixel outside the image is lit** (`FromLamp` gives it a depth of 0): past a depth's edge both reads give the pixels at the edge, so a caster across the edge would smear over everything beyond it, which an image of 22 m showed.
  - **In shadow** the lamp's light and its shine go, and `Lit` adds a little blue there (`dusk`), picked so that the floor in shadow is the paint the flattened shadows were: a pixel of it is (39, 43, 60) of 255 in a picture from before and in one from after, beside (77, 84, 103) in the light. A face that looks away from the lamp gets none of the lamp's light, so it's the same in a shadow and out of one, and whatever the lamp's image says of a caster's far side shows nowhere. The line of light along the floor's edge is its own light, where a flattened shadow dimmed it.
  - **Nothing of the match changed:** the pins are what they were, in debug, release, web-debug and web-release, and a run with no window is the same crates in the same places, frame for frame, before and after.
  - **Measured** (2026-10-07, release, an RTX 4080 SUPER, three runs each taken in turn with the build before, which agreed to the hundredth but where a range is given):
    - A frame's views with 2,345 crates standing (`sumo_costs`): 0.16 ms before and 0.15 to 0.17 after, for 33 commands in the draw list where there were 29; under Node 0.18, and 0.17 to 0.18.
    - Draw calls, by the stats' drawer in a run with no window: 21 to 22 with a ring standing and four balls, 25 to 26 in a fight.
    - The drawer's `gpu` line on OpenGL 3.3, a ring standing and then a fight: 0.12 ms to 0.11 and 0.10 to 0.10 at 1920 by 1080; 0.07 to 0.08 and 0.05 to 0.08 at the docs' 376 by 400; 0.15 to 0.12 for both at that size with a display scale of 3. The lamp's pass costs what it costs whatever the window's size; the flattened shadows ran a frag that samples the floor five times over every crate's shadow, each over the others', which grew with the window.
    - On WebGPU, in headless Chrome on the same GPU with timestamp queries, at 1920 by 1080: 0.20 to 0.20 with a ring standing, 0.19 to 0.15 in a fight (two runs each, of matches that differ, as a page's seed does).
    - With no GPU (Chrome's software rasterizers, 1000 by 640, a run each): WebGL 2 at 18 frames a second before and after, and WebGPU from 50 ms a frame to 55.
  - Not done: an image that follows the ring or the camera, for finer pixels late in a round. Android wasn't run, nor OpenGL on a Mac, which nothing runs.
- **Sumo uses none of what the Box3D package gained on 2026-10-06 yet** (parts, a type that changes, questions, `Inside`, `Hit`, tuning: see Box3D): it landed while they were being built, and adopting them is still to do. What they'd take the place of: a ball that turns heavy is destroyed and spawned again with a bigger `Sphere` (`Gain`, `Roll`), where its sphere could be a part that's swapped under a ball that stays; a tile that falls is a static body destroyed and a loose one spawned in its place (`Crumble`: "a body's type is read once"), where its type could change; how hard things met is worked out from the speed each ball had before the step (`Ball.before`, in `Bump`, `Fuse`, `Knock` and what `Smash` shows), which `Hit` says; and a blast looks at every loose body for the ones in reach (`Blasts`), which `Overlap` answers.
  - **Its two pins were taken again with the package, and its physics are as they were.** What moved them is the engine's, not the package's: each bot's `Brain` has a `Random`, a `Random` field is seeded with its component's number among all the program's components (the last argument of `tide_random_seed_place` in generated C), and a package's components come before a game's, so the package gaining one (`Part`) gave every bot other dice (2560 to 2816 for `Brain.dice`). The old package with one empty component added plays `sumo_bots_stay_on_and_fight_and_the_next_round_is_raised` to the same numbers as the new one, crate for crate. So a package that gains a component changes the match of every game with a `Random` in a component, which a game's author can't see coming: reported to the owner, and not changed here.
- **What they use of the packages,** which their pages say with the code: static, kinematic and dynamic bodies; every shape, several on a body; an impulse and an angular one where a body is spawned, and later from a system that's `[Before(PhysicsStages.Step)]` (Towers' gust, Sumo's drive, its charge and its blasts); `bullet`, damping and `gravityScale`; `Touched` between solid bodies, and a sensor's `Touched` and `Parted`; `Physics.gravity` written during play; a body's position written to put it elsewhere, with `Snap`; entities destroyed. Towers also, of what the Box2D package gained on 2026-10-06: a shape as a `Part`, shapes added to a body that's there, a body's `type` written (kinematic to dynamic as a brick lands, dynamic to kinematic and back for Cement), `Cast` and `Overlap` from a system before the step and from a view, `Hit`, and the world's tuning. It uses no sensor and hears no `Touched` any more. Sumo's are boxes and spheres, static and dynamic: thousands of bodies that sleep until they're hit; an impulse every tick as a force (`Drive`); a body replaced where its type or its shape would have to change, which are read once (a tile that falls is destroyed and a loose one spawned in its place, and so is a ball that grows heavy); impulses written into other entities' bodies through the `World`, from a query, only in a tick that has a blast (`Blasts`); `Touched` between balls for the bump; a sensor under everything that takes what falls (`Pit`) and small ones that are picked up; `Physics.gravity` set once, to 18.
- **Tests:** `towers_tests` and `sumo_tests` (`test/towers_test.c`, `test/sumo_test.c`), with no window. Each checks what its game promises on one world; runs its views and local handlers a frame at a time, as Duel's test does, for where the lobby leaves a machine by how it started (nothing said, a match that's open, joining, a match that ended, a code nobody has) and for the effects an event makes; and plays a match on the loopback network, 40 ms each way with jitter and 5% of packets lost: a server and three clients, of whom one joins in the middle of a round, with commands sent meanwhile. No client may be sent the world again (`resyncs == 0`, the late ones' too), and the server's world is pinned twice, mid-match and after a Reset: the same hashes in debug, release, web-debug and web-release. A change to a game's match moves its pins, and so does one to its package or to the netcode: update them from one platform, then confirm the others agree. Towers' tests have a tower over the line by putting its plinth where the tower's top would be (a kinematic body goes where its position is written), and get towers to test with by having every seat build by itself for a while, then stopping the bots and letting everything come to rest (`grow`). A gust is tested on one brick put on each plinth by hand: the tops bots build are too interlocked for a gust to move a brick far enough to check. `towers_a_falling_brick_keeps_out_of_what_is_in_its_way` steers one into a brick beside it and asks another to turn where it has no room. Its match's late joiner also has to be no more than 15 ticks ahead of what's confirmed a second after it's in.
