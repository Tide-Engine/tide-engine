# CRACKED in Tide: what was ported, and from where

The owner asked on 2026-10-11 for a port of their Unity game CRACKED
(`Purrguelike`, Unity 6 with PurrNet and Purrdiction;
<https://store.steampowered.com/app/3917010/CRACKED/>): players who join a
match through the `rooms` package, maps that rotate, upgrades and the rest,
without the modding scene. This file says where each part of the port comes
from, which numbers were read from the Unity project and which are
approximations, and what isn't ported.

The source of truth was the Unity project itself: its C# under
`Assets/_Scripts`, and the values serialized in its prefabs, scenes and
assets. Nothing here was tuned by feel.

## Where the port differs, and why

- **Collision is the port's own, not PhysX.** A map's colliders are boxes
  (every collider in the rotation is a box, but four trapezoid roofs and
  Map6's baked floor, below), a player is a capsule and a bullet a ball, so
  `collide.tide` answers a sphere cast, a ray and an overlap from distances
  to boxes, and pushes a capsule out of what it's in. Bullets and the ground
  check ask what the Unity game asks PhysX, with its layer rules. A player's
  body has PhysX's order (forces, gravity, damping, then contacts) with no
  friction and no bounce, which is the prefab's physics material, but it's a
  capsule put outside what it's in, not a solved contact. The damping is
  `v *= 1 - damping * dt`, which is what PhysX is remembered to do; that
  wasn't checked against PhysX's source.
- **A dead body tips over; it isn't a ragdoll.** In Unity the body's own
  spin and its contacts make it fall. Here a body that dies is given a spin
  the way it was going and stops when it's flat. How a living body wobbles
  when it's hit (the impulse at the hit's place, and the upright spring) is
  the Unity game's, on a cylinder's inertia where PhysX has a capsule's.
- **The blocks that can be pushed stand still** (Map4's 13 and Map6's 43:
  `EnvironmentPhysics`). They're solid, and bullets bounce off them, but a
  hit doesn't move them and the floor under the map never takes them. They
  need a rigid body solver; `Block` in `state.tide` and each piece's `block`
  are there for it.
- **Four trapezoid roofs are boxes**, of their mesh's bounds, where Unity
  has a convex mesh. They're on those blocks.
- **Map6's floor is 13 boxes**, the slab and its six holes exactly, where
  Unity has a 164,000-triangle bake of the same shape.
- **Bots have no navmesh.** The Unity game's bot follows a baked navmesh
  path to the nearest enemy. This one walks straight at them and jumps where
  the floor ends. Its aim (the low arc that reaches feet, body or head), its
  firing in spells and its block before the floor are the Unity bot's.
- **Random numbers are Tide's.** The Unity game's `PredictedRandom` isn't
  reproduced, so no roll matches its number for number: spread, card deals,
  spawn order, map order.
- **Players come and go.** The Unity game starts from a lobby with a fixed
  roster and gives a player ten seconds to come back. Here a match is always
  running: a player who joins has a seat in the team with fewer players (a
  bot's avatar as it is, when that team has a bot), and one who leaves frees
  theirs at once. A team nobody plays for has one bot, as the Unity game's
  bot mode gives a player alone.
- **After the last round a new game starts by itself**, 12 seconds on. The
  Unity game goes back to its menu.
- **A bullet that's done is gone at once.** The Unity game keeps it three
  seconds for its effects and for rollback; here an event says what happened.
- **An aim that's no direction is repaired.** The input's `Sanitize` clamps
  the aim before it's normalized, where the Unity game only normalizes: a
  huge one there is a bullet that never moves.
- **The match's dice are seeded from its seed** (`Begin` in `game.tide`).
  What Tide would seed them with by itself goes by where the singleton is
  among the program's, which another file can change.
- **A jump pad throws once as a player comes in.** In Unity both of a
  player's colliders enter its trigger, a tick or so apart; each zeroes the
  speed upward and pushes, which comes to one throw.

## Kept as the Unity game has them, odd or not

- A bullet is gone at its first hit unless a card adds bounces: it dies when
  its count of bounces reaches `maxBounces`, which is 1.
- Damage is rounded toward zero (`CeilToInt` of what's taken away), so 4.5
  takes 4.
- A second block (Echo Guard) counts its 0.35 s from when the first ends.
- The shield's `actualActiveTimer` is never reset, so a bullet stops
  refreshing a shield once it has been up 1.8 s in total since the spawn.
- `PlayerMovement.Knockback` gives its random nudge at the world's
  (0, 1.5, 0), not on the body, so how much a throw spins a player depends
  on where in the map they are.
- Two players who fire in the same tick roll their bullets from the same
  seed (the tick's number).
- `BounceDamageReset` and the other modifiers no card uses aren't ported:
  the port has the 17 bullet modifiers, 2 shoot modifiers and 8 effects the
  database's 38 cards use.

## Not ported

The modding scene, the map editor, Steam lobbies and matchmaking, skins and
hats, the tutorial hints, spectator controls (the camera follows by itself),
the unused maps (Map3, Map8, MapCircleSucky) and the lobby's settings
(rounds to win, the floor's damage, mods).
